# Verified environmental sources for PeruRoad Climate

Research date: **17 October 2026, America/Lima**. Discovery and page/PDF verification used **Tavily MCP search and extraction**. Geographic priority: the Rímac basin and PE-22 from Lima through Chosica, Matucana, San Mateo, Ticlio and La Oroya, with the Mantaro extension toward Huancayo. National products are retained only with their geographic limits stated.

The registry now contains **39 official source records**, covering all six requested topics. The initial source pass logged 30 Tavily searches and 18 extraction calls; later follow-ups added 58 and 48 searches, source-catalogue checks, and the notice sweep and corridor audit. The 17 October headline/problem review added 73 advanced searches and 27 extraction calls, and registered two current CENEPRED scenario records. The conservative cumulative estimate is **688 credits** against the participant's **2,000-credit cap**; Tavily did not expose actual billing. Source records include directories and historical references, not 39 live APIs.

## Dated evidence and exclusions

- **New CENEPRED summer-2027 release (7 October):** the official national scenario identifies 702 districts at very high mass-movement risk and 8,695,261 residents across them. It reports 63,290 km of road network and 3,610,747 ha of agricultural land within areas of greater mass-movement susceptibility. These are national scenario figures, not PE-22 counts or active incidents. [Official release](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/cenepred/noticias/1454178-cenepred-presenta-el-estudio-de-escenarios-de-riesgo-por-lluvias-para-el-verano-2027)
- **Water-deficit scenario, September 2026:** CENEPRED identifies 397 districts nationally at very high risk, including 18 in Junín and 10 in Pasco. This is a seasonal scenario and does not establish a current water shortage or farm impact. [Official report](https://sigrid.cenepred.gob.pe/sigridv3/storage/biblioteca/22787_estudio-de-escenario-de-riesgo-por-deficit-hidrico-para-el-periodo-de-lluvias-2026-2027-actualizacion-setiembre.pdf)

- **Upcoming local-relevance rain forecast:** SENAMHI 422 was issued on **16 October** and runs from **18 October 14:00 to 19 October 23:59 Lima**. It explicitly lists corridor provinces. Its regional rain ranges and daily maps need spatial review before attributing a segment; a forecast does not establish that rain fell or a road closed.
- **24-hour rain notice:** SENAMHI 290 starts **17 October 13:00**, with a stated duration of **24 hours**. Its central/northern sierra regional range is **5–20 mm/day**. The actual SHAPE ZIP link was extracted from the official page, but the polygon contents were not verified; do not assume that a page parameter `dp=junin` establishes PE-22 coverage.
- **Local historical frost forecast:** SENAMHI 398 was issued **3 October**, valid **5 October 00:00 to 7 October 23:59**. Junín, Yauli, Jauja, Concepción and Huancayo are explicitly included. This is historical forecast evidence outside the earlier 10–17 October road-incident window.
- **Cold warning rejected for this corridor:** notice 410, valid **13–14 October**, has a title mentioning the central/southern sierra, but its extracted daily department lists do not include Lima or Junín. It is also expired. A broad title cannot establish local coverage.
- **Debris-flow warning rejected for this corridor:** [COEN bulletin 191](https://portal.indeci.gob.pe/emergencias/boletin-informativo-de-aviso-de-corto-plazo-ante-posible-activacion-de-quebradas-n191-17-10-2026/) is real and valid **17 October 13:00 to 18 October 13:00**. Its extracted exposed-province table identifies **Condorcanqui, Amazonas**. It provides no established PE-22 coverage; image-only map content remains a review limit.
- **Pest forecasts are expired and not detections:** insect/disease documents linked from the extracted SENAMHI catalogue end **15 October**. They use experimental weather-based suitability models, including GFS at about **27 km**. Retrieving them on 17 October does not renew their validity or confirm an infestation.
- **New station-level crop evidence:** SENAMHI's report for **6–12 October** records severe maize damage at Huayao after a reported frost reached **−3.5 C**; the event day and field polygon are absent. It also reports artichoke at Ingenio in good condition after an earlier-October frost, and 2.5 mm rain with irrigated potato at Comas. The separate 2 October SENAMHI release reports burned maize leaves in the Mantaro Valley.
- **New observed rainfall:** INDECI's 17 October daily bulletin records **12.0 mm at Ingenio station** in Concepción, Junín, from **16 October 07:00 to 17 October 07:00**, classified as a rainy day. The atlas point is rounded to 0.1 degree. This is real local station data, but it is not a road or field measurement.
- **New agricultural emergency evidence:** INDECI report 14162 states a forest fire began at **Ayas, Surco, Huarochirí** on **30 September at 12:49**. Its damage table, updated 2 October, records 45 ha of agricultural crops affected and 25 ha of natural cover destroyed; the report says the fire was extinguished and later closed. No exact point coordinate is published.
- **Notice geometry attempt:** SENAMHI notice 422 is valid on **18–19 October** for named Lima/Junín provinces and forecasts rain, hail, snow and strong gusts. Its advertised SHAPE-ZIP/WFS links were tested through Tavily extraction and returned no extractable content, so no polygon or PE-22 intersection is claimed.
- **Additional notices checked and excluded:** short-term INDECI rain bulletins 277 and 279 were valid **5–8 October** and have expired. INDECI fire-conditions bulletin 010 was valid **11–16 October**; its text forecasts favorable fire-weather conditions mainly over the southern Andes, but the extracted exposure table did not establish Huarochirí/Junín coverage. None is a current map alert.
- **Local town forecasts:** the SENAMHI pages issued **16 October** forecast light showers at Casapalca, a light-rain tendency at Matucana and light/moderate rain at La Oroya over **17–19 October**. A separate **−2 C** minimum at Yanacancha on 17 October is a forecast, not an observed frost. Forecasts remain town-level and do not establish conditions on a road segment.
- **Future water and hazard context:** SENAMHI's October 2026–February 2027 seasonal hydrology report projects central Pacific flows, including the Rímac, initially near normal and above normal from December. CENEPRED's summer 2027 scenario lists 363 mass-movement critical zones in Lima and 75 in Junín, plus 302 and 128 flood critical points respectively; these are department aggregates, not mapped PE-22 intersections.
- **Current short-term quebrada notice:** INDECI bulletin 191 is valid **17 October 13:00 to 18 October 13:00** and is based on SENAMHI notice 290. Its extracted local corridor coverage remains unresolved, so it is not used as a PE-22 hazard pin.
- **Historical studies retain uncertainty:** the Pucruchacra/San Mateo study locates a critical area at road **km 93+000**, but its 2011 event-day statements conflict. It is background evidence; the newly discovered 2026 report failed extraction and was not admitted as verified content.

Observed crop frost damage at Huayao, a measured rainy-day total at Ingenio, and closed crop damage from the Ayas wildfire are now verified in official reports. The search did not establish a current PE-22 road disruption, flood/landslide occurrence or local pest detection. A missing event in this research is not evidence that no hazard occurred.

## Real station references for the project

The extracted [CHIRILU bulletin 3](https://www.senamhi.gob.pe/load/file/02269SENA-112.pdf) contains station references for Ñaña, Chosica, Matucana, San Mateo de Huanchor, Río Blanco, Casapalca and Marcapomacocha. The September monitoring bulletin 04 gives Casapalca rainfall; the ENFEN September report gives Chosica and San Mateo river-flow summaries; INDECI bulletin 290 gives a 12.0 mm daily total for Ingenio on 16–17 October. These are dated observations, while the CHIRILU live table remains unavailable through Tavily. [The station-atlas annex](https://www.senamhi.gob.pe/pdf/anexos-atlas-climatico.pdf) adds reference coordinates for Huayao, Ingenio and La Oroya, but several are rounded to 0.1 degree.

The bulletin covers **21–30 September 2026** and presents **1991–2020** climate normals. It says the next update is 10 October. Preserve these dates: an accessible bulletin is not automatically the latest monitoring period.

The existing `_christian` weekly road collector still handles SUTRAN, INDECI and Google News. This research does not silently add crop/pest or weather forecasts to its confirmed road incidents. The new registry is an evidence-backed handoff; access to protected station downloads, archive contents, polygon intersection and live measurement APIs remains unimplemented or unverified where stated.

Recommended collection cadence is an implementation proposal: inspect weather warning catalogues daily; collect hydrological/station observations only through a verified access route; refresh seasonal crop/CENEPRED products when reissued; version INGEMMET layers by their real update date; and require official confirmation before publishing a pest occurrence. Discover links from source catalogues rather than constructing the next PDF/ZIP filename.

SENAMHI detail pages can include many other warning panels before the requested one. Select the exact warning number before extracting its dates, severity, province lists or WFS links. Never join one panel's geography to another panel's forecast text.

Every record used by the product must preserve its distinction among observed event/measurement, official forecast, experimental forecast, historical inventory, susceptibility map and source directory. Missing values stay null; inaccessible or undated material stays in review. No visualization files or shared configuration were changed by this research.
