# PeruRoad Climate: Outamina problem definition and notice audit

Research date: **17 October 2026, America/Lima**. Searches and document checks used Tavily. This is a source review and data handoff, not a visualization change. It distinguishes current notices, observations, past events, pest surveillance, and seasonal risk scenarios.

## Recommended problem definition

### Client framing and geographic boundary

The project owner identifies **Outamina** as the client and **mining companies** as the intended customer sector. A Tavily check for the exact name did not verify an authoritative Outamina company page, mine, or operating corridor; search results were liable to conflate the name with Antamina. This brief therefore accepts the client identity as project-provided information but does not equate Outamina with Antamina or claim any specific mine location, route, or asset.

The shared team plan still describes PeruRoad Climate as a PE-22/Carretera Central product with drivers and dispatchers as its primary users and a Mantaro agricultural extension. That geography is not yet verified as serving Outamina. Research below remains usable as evidence about the named places and dates, but none of the PE-22 findings should be represented as an Outamina-site alert until the client’s mine, access routes, and relevant territory are confirmed. This memo follows the latest client direction; the shared plan is unchanged.

SUTRAN already publishes an official current road-status map. For Outamina’s mining customers, the proposed gap is joining confirmed road/access status with time-valid weather, hydrology, and geohazard information for the specific mine, access corridor, and operating window, while clearly distinguishing a forecast from an observation, a susceptibility study, or a confirmed restriction. Where relevant to mine operations and the surrounding territory, the same evidence view should include frost, agriculture, water availability, and dated pest surveillance. PeruRoad Climate can complement the [SUTRAN status map](https://gis.sutran.gob.pe/alerta_sutran) by joining such sources; it must not present susceptibility or seasonal scenarios as live incidents or road closures.

**Mining-company operations teams need one location- and time-specific view linking weather, water, slope hazards, and verified road/access status so they can assess accident exposure, access closures, and interruptions to worker and supply movements.** The relevant evidence is split across road-status alerts, SENAMHI weather and hydrology notices, INDECI emergency reports, CENEPRED/INGEMMET hazard studies, and, where local production and communities matter, agricultural and SENASA plant-health information. Those sources use different places, timestamps, update cycles, and levels of certainty. A province-wide rain forecast does not prove that a mine road is blocked; a critical-zone inventory does not mean a huaico is happening now; a crop forecast or pest-surveillance route is not a confirmed pest detection. This is a project problem statement to validate with Outamina, not a claim that the company has reported each of these failures.

**PeruRoad Climate should join dated, source-linked signals to mine-operation decisions while keeping evidence types separate.** Proposed users include mining logistics/dispatch teams deciding whether to release, delay, or reroute vehicles; HSE/geotechnical teams deciding whether a stretch or facility needs inspection; and environmental/water teams tracking rainfall, hydrology, frost, and water availability. Agricultural and pest information belongs when a dated, local source makes it relevant to mine-adjacent communities, suppliers, or territorial coordination. Each view must identify the source, place, issue time, validity, geometry precision, and whether it is an observation, forecast, risk scenario, historical impact, or surveillance activity. These user roles and decisions are proposed personas to confirm with Outamina.

For a mining-focused MVP, prioritize **confirmed road/access status and active, spatially applicable environmental notices** for a verified mine and its access corridors. Keep water, frost, rain, landslide/mudslide, agriculture, and pest records in scope, but show each only at the spatial and temporal precision the source supports. Do not use the current PE-22 map as a proxy for Outamina’s operating footprint unless the client confirms that connection. The exact site/corridor is the key unresolved input before claiming mine-specific coverage or automating operational recommendations.

## Why access disruptions are credible—and why corridor evidence cannot yet be assigned to Outamina

The records below document real hazards, road emergencies, and seasonal exposure in Peru. They do not establish Outamina's mine locations or its exposure. Until the client footprint is confirmed, the corridor-specific findings are contextual evidence rather than client-specific proof.

- Provías Nacional reported **918 emergencies on the non-concessioned national road network in January–June 2026**. It said 827 had been resolved and 91 remained under attention at publication; one had interrupted traffic and 90 had restricted it. These are national road-maintenance figures, not PE-22 counts. [Provías Nacional, 8 July 2026](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/pvn/noticias/1416357-provias-nacional-atendio-mas-de-900-emergencias-viales-en-la-red-vial-nacional-durante-el-primer-semestre-del-2026)
- An official INDECI Huarochirí report from February 2023 records road damage and agricultural, irrigation, and water-network impacts across named districts. It establishes that weather-related emergencies can affect both access and livelihoods in the wider Lima highlands; it does **not** establish that every listed impact was on PE-22. [INDECI complementary report 1955](https://portal.indeci.gob.pe/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/REPORTE-COMPLEMENTARIO-N%C2%BA-1955-24FEB2023-HUAICO-EN-LA-PROVINCIA-DE-HUAROCHIRI-LIMA-13.pdf)
- CENEPRED's 7 October 2026 summer-2027 scenario identifies 702 districts at very high risk from mass movements, with 8,695,261 residents across those districts. Within areas of greater susceptibility it reports 3,333,277 residents, 63,290 km of road network, and 3,610,747 ha of agricultural land. These are national scenario/exposure figures for January–March 2027, not current incidents or PE-22 measurements. [CENEPRED's official release](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/cenepred/noticias/1454178-cenepred-presenta-el-estudio-de-escenarios-de-riesgo-por-lluvias-para-el-verano-2027)
- A separate September 2026 CENEPRED water-deficit scenario classifies 397 districts nationally at very high risk, including 18 in Junín and 10 in Pasco. It estimates 2,466,388 ha of agricultural land and 8,024,224 ha of pasture across all very-high-risk districts. This is a seasonal risk scenario, not evidence of a measured local shortage. [CENEPRED/SIGRID report](https://sigrid.cenepred.gob.pe/sigridv3/storage/biblioteca/22787_estudio-de-escenario-de-riesgo-por-deficit-hidrico-para-el-periodo-de-lluvias-2026-2027-actualizacion-setiembre.pdf)
- The current SENAMHI notice 422 is a better example of an actionable environmental notice: issued 16 October and valid **18 October 14:00–19 October 23:59**, it names Huarochirí and Yauli as well as Junín highland provinces and forecasts rain, hail/snow at elevation, and strong gusts. The notice geometry could not be verified in this research, so province inclusion does not prove a particular road segment is affected. [SENAMHI notice 422](https://www.senamhi.gob.pe/?p=aviso-meteorologico-vigente&a=2026&b=29287&c=00&d=SENA)

Before this review, the 20-record map handoff already contained this forecast, a measured **12.0 mm** Ingenio rainfall total for 16–17 October, and an official report of severe frost damage to maize at Huayao during 6–12 October. These are valid information records, but some have no defensible event geometry. A quiet map at this time of year is therefore not a reason to place an unrelated national count or a district-centre guess on the road.

## Data and automation rules for this problem

For each record, retain the issuing agency, source URL, publication/update time, event time, validity window, hazard or crop, affected jurisdiction, road code/km when supplied, geometry source and precision, observed/forecast/scenario/surveillance type, and status. Expire short-term notices by their stated end time; preserve old reports in history. A news item can discover an official source but cannot fill missing fields from inference.

Only create a map point from an official coordinate, a route code plus a verifiable kilometre marker, or an inspected official polygon. For province-only or national records, use a dated context panel or table and keep geometry null. Do not assign a district centroid, turn seasonal exposure into an active incident, infer a road closure from rainfall, or turn pest suitability/surveillance into an outbreak. Each road-closure display needs an authority and a status timestamp; each agricultural notice needs its crop and season.

**Why the current PE-22 handoff can still look sparse:** it has 22 evidence records but only 9 defensible point geometries; 13 forecasts, scenarios, or reports lack a precise coordinate. This review adds two official seasonal context records without adding pins. In the pre-rainy season, a low count of active local disasters is a truthful state. The product can still show its value through source-linked forecasts, measured station readings, past impacts, and background susceptibility—clearly labeled as different evidence. This count describes the existing PE-22/Mantaro research only; because Outamina's operating footprint is unverified, it says nothing about notice coverage or risk around an Outamina mine.
